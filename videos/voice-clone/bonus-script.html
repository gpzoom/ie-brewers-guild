Same setup as the main script

  • Same closet, mic, distance and Audacity settings. Start each file with 5 seconds of silence.
  • Session B3 is conversational. Read it like you're really talking to someone: excited, sorry, surprised, thinking. That range helps the clone sound natural.
  • Ellipses (...) mean a short, natural pause.
  • Name the files bonus1.wav through bonus4.wav.

Session B1 · Getting to know the Guild

Paragraphs B1–B12 · about 4 minutes☐ Recorded
B1

Hi there, and thanks for stopping by. If you've ever wondered who's behind the local beer scene, you're in the right place. The Guild is a group of independent brewers, taprooms, and the people who support them. We're neighbors first, and businesses second.

B2

So what does the Guild actually do? Three big things. We speak up for small brewers when laws and rules are being made. We help the public learn what makes local beer worth seeking out. And we bring people together, at festivals, at tastings, and at meetups throughout the year.

B3

You might be surprised how many breweries are close to home. Some are big enough to fill a warehouse. Others are tucked into a strip mall between a nail salon and a pizza place. Every one of them has a story, and most of them are happy to tell it.

B4

If you're a brewery owner, joining the Guild puts you on the map. Literally. Your profile appears on the Guild website, right alongside your neighbors. People planning a day out can see who's open, what's pouring, and what's happening this weekend.

B5

And it isn't only for breweries. Allied members, like hop farms, glass printers, and equipment suppliers, have profiles too. So do mobile businesses, like food trucks and beer bikes. If you help make the local beer scene better, there's a place for you here.

B6

Let me show you around the homepage. At the top, there's a big photo and a short welcome. Just below it, you'll see the calendar pages. Each page is a member event happening in the next two weeks. Watch for a moment, and the page tears away to show the next one.

B7

Want to stop the pages from flipping? Press Pause. Want them to start again? Press Play. You can also move your mouse away, and they'll pick up right where they left off.

B8

Scroll a little further, and you'll find the member directory. You can search by name, or filter by what you're looking for. Breweries. Taprooms. Brewpubs. Allied members. Mobile businesses. Each card opens a full profile.

B9

Every profile tells you the basics at a glance. Is it open right now? When does it close? Where is it? And what's coming up? If you want to go deeper, keep scrolling. You'll find photos, beers, food, and the story behind the place.

B10

Planning a visit with friends? Here's a tip. Open a few profiles in different tabs, and compare the hours. Then pick two or three places close together. You'll spend less time driving, and more time enjoying yourself. And please, always plan a safe ride home.

B11

We keep adding to the website, based on what members and visitors tell us. If something's confusing, or if there's a feature you wish we had, let us know. Some of our best ideas started as a quick note from someone just like you.

B12

Okay, that's the quick tour. In the next part, we'll roll up our sleeves and get into a few more tasks in the Member Portal. Don't worry, none of them take very long.

Session B2 · More in the Member Portal

Paragraphs B13–B24 · about 4 minutes☐ Recorded
B13

Let's start with your team. Running a brewery is never a one-person job, so you don't have to keep your profile up to date alone. Open People in the Member Portal, and press Invite. Type an email address, and choose what that person can do. Then press Send.

B14

The person you invited gets an email with a link. When they open it, they'll set up their own sign-in. From then on, they can make changes to your profile. You can remove them anytime, with one press of a button.

B15

Did someone leave your team? Go to People, find their name, and press Remove. They'll lose access right away. It's a good habit to check this list every few months, just to make sure it's current.

B16

Now, what about your photos? If you've been adding photos from your phone, here's a trick. Use the upload link. It's a special web address that opens straight to your photo gallery. Save it to your phone's home screen, and you can add pictures from the taproom in seconds.

B17

You can even share that link with a trusted photographer. They can upload their photos directly, without needing a password. And if you ever want to turn the link off, you can do that too. Nothing they upload goes live until you've had a look.

B18

Your beer list changes all the time, doesn't it? One week you've got eight beers on tap. The next week, three of them are gone and two new ones are pouring. Here's the fastest way to keep up. Open Beers, and switch each one on or off. That's it. No need to delete anything.

B19

Coming up on a holiday weekend? Open Hours, and scroll to Special hours. Add the dates, and set your hours for each one. Maybe you're closed on the holiday itself, and open late the night before. Special hours only apply to those dates, then everything goes back to normal.

B20

What if you're closing for a week, for vacation or a remodel? Add each of those days in Special hours, and mark them closed. Your profile will say Closed today on each of those days, and it'll show when you're opening again.

B21

Let's talk about your description once more. Short paragraphs are easier to read on a phone. Start with what makes you different. Then tell people what to expect when they walk in. End with something inviting, like, Come see us, or, Pull up a stool.

B22

Something not showing up the way you expected? Here are three things to try. First, make sure you pressed Save. Second, refresh the page. Third, check the preview. If it's right in the preview, it'll be right on the website.

B23

Still stuck? That's what we're here for. Press Help, and send us a short message. Tell us what you were trying to do, and what happened instead. A screenshot helps, too. We'll get back to you as quickly as we can, usually the same day.

B24

And remember, you can't break anything by exploring. Open a section, look around, and press Cancel if you change your mind. The more you poke around, the more comfortable you'll get.

Session B4 · Pairings, numbers, and stories

Paragraphs B39–B52 · about 4 minutes☐ Recorded
B39

Let's talk about food and beer together. A crisp lager is great with anything fried or salty. Think fries, tacos, or fried chicken. The bubbles cut right through the richness, and get you ready for the next bite.

B40

An IPA loves spicy food. The hops and the heat play off each other. Try it with buffalo wings, a spicy curry, or a plate of street tacos with plenty of salsa. Just be ready to order a second glass of water.

B41

A stout is made for dessert. Chocolate cake. Vanilla ice cream. A warm brownie. The roasty flavors of the beer bring out the sweetness of the dessert, and suddenly you're not sure which one you like better.

B42

And what about cheese? A good rule is to match strength with strength. A mild cheese goes with a light beer. A sharp cheddar goes with an amber or an IPA. And a stinky blue cheese can stand up to a big barleywine.

B43

Here are some numbers from our last festival. Forty-two breweries poured more than one hundred sixty beers. About three thousand five hundred people came through the gates. And together, we raised eighteen thousand dollars for a local food bank.

B44

Tickets go on sale Monday, March second, at ten a.m. Early bird tickets are thirty-five dollars through March fifteenth. After that, they're forty-five. Designated driver tickets are just ten dollars, and they include free soda and snacks all day.

B45

Our summer hours start on June first. Monday through Thursday, we're open from two to ten. Friday and Saturday, we're open from eleven a.m. to midnight. And on Sunday, we're open from eleven to eight. Happy hour is every weekday from three to five.

B46

A standard beer barrel holds thirty-one gallons. That's about two hundred forty-eight pints. A half barrel keg, the kind you see at parties, holds fifteen and a half gallons. And a sixtel, the small one, holds about five gallons.

B47

When I first started brewing, I made beer in my kitchen on a Saturday morning. I burned the first batch. The second one was flat. The third one was actually pretty good, and I was hooked. Twelve years later, I still get excited every time we open a new tank.

B48

There's an old saying in brewing. Brewers make wort, and yeast makes beer. It means that the brewer does the careful work at the start, but then you have to trust the process, step back, and let nature do its part.

B49

Every brewery has a favorite regular. Ours comes in every Thursday at five fifteen, sits in the same seat, and orders the same pilsner. We once changed the recipe, just a little, and he noticed on the first sip. We changed it back the very next week.

B50

Some of the best beer ideas start as mistakes. A batch left too long in a barrel. A fruit that showed up by accident. A recipe written down wrong. Once in a while, those mistakes become a favorite that people ask for every single year.

B51

Here's what I love most about this community. When one brewery runs out of hops, another one lends them some. When someone's walk-in cooler breaks down, a neighbor makes room in theirs. We compete for customers, sure, but we look out for each other.

B52

If you've listened all the way to the end, thank you. Whether you brew beer, pour it, sell it, or just love drinking it, you're part of what makes the local scene special. So raise a glass, find a new place to try, and tell them the Guild sent you. Cheers.

End of bonus script
