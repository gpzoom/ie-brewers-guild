Voice Clone Training Script

About 3,800 words · 25–30 minutes at a relaxed pace · five sessions of 7–10 minutes each

Before you record

  • Same room, mic, distance and settings for every session.
  • Quiet, soft room. A closet full of clothes is ideal. Turn off fans, AC and the fridge if you can.
  • A hand's width from the mic, speaking slightly past it, not straight into it.
  • No noise removal, music or "enhance" filters. Upload the raw recording.
  • Read like you're helping one person, with a slight smile. Let every sentence land. Don't trail off.
  • Flubbed a line? Pause, then re-read the whole paragraph. You can trim mistakes later.
  • Send a 1-minute test file first so the levels and room noise can be checked before you record it all.
  • Check the box beside each paragraph as you finish it, so you can find your place next session.

Session 1 · Welcome and your profile

Paragraphs 1–15 · about 8 minutes☐ Recorded
1

Welcome to the Guild. We're glad you're here. Over the next few minutes, I'll show you how to set up your profile, step by step. There's nothing to memorize, and nothing you can break. If you get stuck, you can always come back to this video and pick up where you left off.

2

Your profile is the page people see when they look you up on the Guild website. It's where they find your hours, your address, your beers, and what's coming up at your place. A complete profile helps people decide to visit. An empty one sends them somewhere else.

3

Let's start by signing in. Go to the Guild website and press Sign in, at the top right of the page. Type your email address and your password, and press Continue. If you've forgotten your password, press Forgot password, and we'll send you a link to make a new one. That's it. You're in.

4

The first thing you'll see is the Member Portal. Think of it as your home base. On the left, there's a list of sections. On the right, there's the section you're working on. Each section gets a checkmark once it's finished, so you always know what's left.

5

Start with the basics. Type the name of your business exactly the way you want it to appear. Then add your street address, your city, and your zip code. Double-check the spelling. A small typo in an address can send someone to the wrong side of town.

6

Next, add a phone number and a website, if you have one. These are optional, but they help. When someone's standing on the sidewalk wondering if you're open, a phone number they can tap is worth a lot.

7

Now for your logo. Press Upload logo, and choose the file from your computer or your phone. A square image works best. If your logo has a white background, that's fine. We'll show it on a clean card so it looks sharp.

8

Do you have a brand color? Maybe it's the green on your cans, or the red on your sign. Choose it here, and we'll use it on your profile and on the Guild homepage. If you're not sure, pick a color you like. You can change it anytime.

9

Here's a tip. Write your description the way you'd talk to a friend at the bar. Skip the marketing words. Tell people what it feels like to walk in. Is it loud and busy? Quiet and cozy? Family friendly? Dog friendly? Say so.

10

When you're done with a section, press Save. You'll see a short message that says Saved. If you try to leave a page without saving, don't worry. We'll ask you first, so nothing gets lost by accident.

11

Let's talk about hours. Open the Hours section. For each day of the week, choose whether you're open or closed. If you're open, set the time you open and the time you close. Monday, closed. Tuesday, closed. Wednesday, three to nine. Thursday, three to nine. Friday, noon to ten. Saturday, noon to ten. Sunday, noon to six.

12

What about holidays? Scroll down to Special hours. Add a date, like Thanksgiving, or the Fourth of July, and choose whether you're closed or open different hours that day. Special hours always win over your regular hours, so you don't have to change your whole week for one day.

13

Why do hours matter so much? Because the first question almost everyone asks is simple. Are you open right now? Your profile answers that question automatically. It says Open now, or Opens at three, or Closed today. But it can only be right if your hours are right.

14

Take a moment and look at what you've done so far. Your name, your address, your logo, your color, and your hours. That's already more than most people ever see in a search result. Nice work.

15

Let's keep going. In the next part, we'll add photos. Good photos are the fastest way to make someone want to visit. You don't need a professional camera. The phone in your pocket is more than enough.

Session 2 · Photos, beers, and publishing

Paragraphs 16–30 · about 8 minutes☐ Recorded
16

Open the Photos section. Press Add photos, and choose a few pictures. You can pick more than one at a time. While they upload, you'll see a small progress bar under each one. When the bar is full, the photo is ready.

17

Which photos should you use? Start with the room. Show people where they'll sit. Then show the bar, the taps, and a few full glasses. Add a picture of the outside, so people recognize the building when they pull up. And if you have a patio, show it off.

18

A few quick tips for better photos. Turn on the lights. Stand near a window if you can. Hold the phone level. Wipe the lens with your shirt. And take ten pictures instead of one, so you can pick the best.

19

You can change the order of your photos by dragging them. The first photo is the one people see first, so make it your favorite. If you change your mind about a photo, press the trash can to remove it.

20

Want to add a caption? Tap a photo and type a short line under it. Something like, Our back patio on a Saturday afternoon. Or, The new fifteen barrel brewhouse. Captions are optional, but they help people understand what they're looking at.

21

Now let's talk about your beers. Open the Beer section and press Add a beer. Type the name, then choose the style. IPA. Pale ale. Pilsner. Stout. Porter. Sour. Lager. Hazy IPA. Amber. Wheat. If you don't see your style, just type it in.

22

Next, add the alcohol by volume. For example, six point five percent. Then add a short description. Keep it simple. What does it taste like? Is it hoppy, malty, crisp, or juicy? Is it light enough for a hot afternoon, or big enough for a cold night?

23

Here's an example. Citrus Street is a hazy IPA at six point eight percent. It's soft and juicy, with notes of orange, mango, and a little pine. It's our most popular beer, and it goes fast, so come early.

24

And another one. Night Shift is a coffee stout at seven point two percent. We brew it with beans from a roaster down the street. It's roasty and smooth, with a finish like dark chocolate. It's perfect with dessert, or as dessert.

25

Is a beer only around for the season? Mark it as seasonal. When it's gone, you can hide it without deleting it. Next year, when it comes back, just turn it back on. Your description and photo will still be there waiting.

26

Do you serve food? Maybe you have a kitchen, or maybe food trucks stop by. Tell people about it. If you work with food trucks, you can even connect a food calendar, so your profile shows who's coming this week.

27

And what if nobody's scheduled? Then your profile says, Bring your own food. That way, visitors are never surprised. They'll know to grab a sandwich on the way, or to have something delivered to the taproom.

28

Let's check your work. Press Preview at the top of the page. This shows you exactly what the public will see. Scroll through it slowly. Does anything look wrong? Is anything missing? If so, go back and fix it. The preview updates as soon as you save.

29

When you're happy with your profile, press Publish. That's the moment it goes live on the Guild website. From then on, any change you save shows up right away. You don't need to publish again.

30

Not ready to publish yet? That's okay. Your work is saved, and only you can see it. Take your time. Come back tomorrow, or next week. Your profile will be right where you left it.

Session 3 · Events and calendars

Paragraphs 31–45 · about 9 minutes☐ Recorded
31

Now let's talk about events. Trivia nights. Live music. Release parties. Food pairings. Anniversary celebrations. Your events can show up on your profile and on the Guild homepage, and it all happens on its own.

32

Here's how it works. You keep your events in a calendar, like Google Calendar. The Guild website reads that calendar every so often and picks up your events. You don't have to type them twice. Add an event to your calendar, and a little later, it's on the website.

33

It takes three steps. Make an events calendar. Tag your events. Then paste one link into the Member Portal. Let's go through them one at a time.

34

Step one. Make a new calendar just for your events. Anyone with its link can see everything on it, so keep it separate from your personal calendar. In Google Calendar, press the plus sign next to Other calendars. Choose Create new calendar. Give it a name, like Taproom Events, and press Create calendar.

35

Next, open the settings for that new calendar. Under Access permissions, check the box that says Make available to public. Right next to it, there's a menu. Change it from See only free busy, to See all event details. This one matters. If you skip it, Google hides your event names, and nothing comes through.

36

Now scroll down to the section called Integrate calendar. Find the line that says Public address in iCal format. Press the copy button next to it. That long link is the one we need.

37

Step two. Add your events to this calendar, the same way you always would. Then, in each event's title or description, type the tag. The tag is hashtag guild. Only events with the tag come through. Everything else stays private to your calendar.

38

Why use a tag at all? Because some events are just for your staff, or for a private party. You don't want those on the website. The tag lets you choose, one event at a time, what the public sees.

39

Step three. Sign in to the Member Portal and open Events. Press Add calendar. Paste the link you copied, and type the same tag. Then press Refresh now. In a moment, your events will appear in the list below.

40

Your events show up on your profile right away, with the date, the time, the place, and your description. If you put a link in the description, like a ticket page or a band's website, people can click it.

41

Events in the next two weeks also appear on the Guild homepage. Each one gets its own calendar page, with your logo and your color. The pages flip, one after another, so visitors can see what's happening all around the region.

42

From now on, just add events to that calendar. The website checks for changes on its own. Google can take a few hours to update, so give it a little time. If you're in a hurry, open the Member Portal and press Refresh now.

43

What if an event gets canceled? Change it in your calendar, or delete it. The next time the website checks, it will update. If you need it gone right away, press Refresh now. And if you want to hide one event without touching your calendar, you can hide it in the Member Portal.

44

What if an event moves to a different day? Just drag it to the new day in your calendar. The website follows your calendar. You never have to fix the same thing in two places.

45

Here's a common question. Can I use my existing calendar instead of making a new one? You can, but we don't recommend it. A public calendar shares everything on it, including dentist appointments and staff meetings. A separate events calendar keeps your private life private.

Who else can edit my profile? You can invite people from your team. Open People in the Member Portal, and type their email address. They'll get an invitation. When they accept, they can help keep your profile up to date.

Visit our website at example brewing dot com. Or find us on the Guild website. Press Members, then type our name in the search box. You can also scan the code on the table in front of you.

On tap today, we have a pilsner, an amber, two IPAs, and a stout. This weekend, there's trivia, a food truck, and live music. To get started, you'll need your email, your logo, and about fifteen minutes.

56

The beer is strong, but it's surprisingly easy to drink. The patio is small, but it's the best seat in the house. The calendar setup looks complicated, but you'll only do it once.

57

The first time I walked into a small brewery, it was raining. The room was warm, and it smelled like bread and hops. Someone at the bar slid over to make room. By the end of the night, I knew half the people there by name. That's what a good taproom does. It turns strangers into regulars.

58

Brewing is part science, and part patience. You measure everything. You clean everything, twice. And then you wait. Sometimes a batch surprises you. Sometimes it's better than you hoped. And every once in a while, it teaches you something you didn't know you needed to learn.

59

The Guild exists because independent brewers are stronger together. We share what we know. We speak up for the things that matter to small businesses. We teach people what makes local beer special. And we celebrate each other's wins, because a good year for one brewery is a good year for all of us.

60

If you're new to the Guild, welcome. If you've been with us for years, thank you. Either way, your profile is how the public meets you. Keep it fresh. Add your events. Post new photos when the seasons change. And update your hours when they change, so nobody shows up to a locked door.

61

Let's finish with a quick recap. Sign in to the Member Portal. Fill in your basics, your hours, and your photos. Add your beers. Connect your events calendar. Preview your profile, and when it looks right, press Publish.

62

Session 5 · Beer talk, tips, and stories

Paragraphs 63–84 · about 10 minutes☐ Recorded
63

Not sure how to describe a beer style? Here's a quick guide. A pilsner is light, crisp, and a little bready, with a clean, snappy finish. It's the beer you want after mowing the lawn. It's simple on the surface, but it's one of the hardest styles to brew well, because there's nowhere for mistakes to hide.

64

An IPA leans on hops. A West Coast IPA is usually clear, bitter, and piney, with a dry finish. A hazy IPA is cloudy and soft, and it tastes more like fruit juice than pine needles. Both are called IPAs, but they feel like two completely different beers.

65

A stout is dark, roasty, and rich. Some taste like coffee. Some taste like chocolate. Some are aged in whiskey barrels and are strong enough to share. And some, like a dry Irish stout, are surprisingly light, even though they look heavy in the glass.

66

A sour is tart and bright, a little like lemonade for grown-ups. Brewers often add fruit, like raspberries, peaches, or passion fruit. If someone tells you they don't like beer, hand them a sour. You might change their mind.

67

Here's something people often ask. What's the difference between a brewery, a brewpub, and a taproom? A brewery makes the beer. A taproom is where you drink it, usually right next to the tanks. And a brewpub makes beer and serves a full menu of food, all under one roof.

68

Let's go back to the Member Portal for a minute. If you ever want to see your profile the way a stranger sees it, open a private window in your browser, go to the Guild website, and search for your name. It's a great way to catch things you missed.

69

Planning a big weekend? Add each event separately. One for Friday's music. One for Saturday's release. One for Sunday's brunch. Separate events are easier to find, and each one gets its own page on the Guild homepage.

70

Is your event a recurring one, like trivia every Tuesday? Set it to repeat in your calendar. The website picks up every Tuesday on its own. And if you skip a week for a holiday, just delete that one date in your calendar. The rest stay put.

71

Write event titles that tell people what to expect. Trivia Night with Prizes is better than Tuesday Fun. Live Music, Blues and Soul, is better than Band. And Anniversary Party, Five Years, is better than Party. A clear title gets more people through the door.

72

In the description, answer the questions people always ask. Is it free? Do I need a ticket? Is it family friendly? Is there food? How late does it go? If you answer them up front, you'll get fewer phone calls, and happier guests.

73

Working with food trucks? Here's how to set that up. Make a second calendar, just for food. Add each truck as an event, with the truck's name in the title and a link to its menu in the description. Then connect that calendar in the Member Portal, the same way you connected your events.

74

Your profile then shows the next seven days. Tuesday, Tacos El Gordo, four to nine. Wednesday, Bring your own food. Thursday, closed. Friday, Smokin' Barrel Barbecue, five to ten. Visitors know exactly what to expect, before they ever leave the house.

75

A quick word about photos from your phone. When you take a picture, your phone saves the original in full quality. If you text a photo to yourself first, it often gets shrunk and blurry. So upload straight from your camera roll whenever you can.

76

Hmm. Is that right? Let me check. Yes, that's the one. Okay, now press Save. Perfect. See the green checkmark? That means it worked.

77

Oh, one more thing before we move on. If you ever see a red message on the screen, read it carefully. It will tell you exactly what's missing. Fix that one thing, press Save again, and you'll be on your way.

78

Honestly, the hardest part is getting started. Once your profile is up, keeping it fresh only takes a few minutes a month. Add a new beer. Swap in a new photo. Put next month's events on the calendar. That's all it takes.

79

Every fall, just after the harvest, brewers get a short window to make fresh hop beer. The hops go from the field to the kettle in a single day. The beer tastes green and grassy and alive, and a few weeks later, it's gone until next year. That's part of what makes it special.

80

Some of the best nights at a taproom aren't planned at all. A neighbor brings a guitar. Someone's birthday turns into a toast for the whole room. Two strangers find out they grew up on the same street. You can't put those on a calendar, but they're the reason people keep coming back.

81

Here's a simple way to think about your profile. It's your front door on the internet. Keep it clean. Keep the lights on. Make it easy to find. And when someone knocks, make sure they know they're welcome.

82

Ready to try it yourself? Great. Pause this video, open the Member Portal in another window, and follow along. Take one step, then come back. There's no rush. We'll be right here.

83

Wow, look at that. Your events are live. Your photos look great. Your hours are right. People can find you, and they'll know exactly when to come by. That's a big step, and you should be proud of it.

84

Thanks again for being part of the Guild. Every profile makes the whole community easier to find, and that helps every brewery in the region. We'll see you out there. Cheers, and happy brewing.
